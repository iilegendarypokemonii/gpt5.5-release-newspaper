Your ChatGPT just got rewired

If you pay for ChatGPT, GPT-5.5 is already in your account. Three things changed in how it works, and one matters before you trust it.

By the automated research deskDateline: T+24 hours

It knows more. It also answers too often when it does not know.

Most daily prompts will feel familiar. Ask for a summary, a rewrite, or a quick explanation and GPT-5.5 will probably feel like a sharper version of what you already use. The difference shows up when the request stops being a chat and starts being a job.

That job might be researching a topic, rewriting a long report, checking citations, inspecting an app, building a prototype, or moving between documents and screens. OpenAI's bet is that GPT-5.5 can hold more context, choose more of the steps itself, and keep going with less hand-holding.

This is why the Codex release around GPT-5.5 matters. Browser use, document viewing, screenshots, plugins, and guarded long runs turn the model upgrade into a workflow upgrade. The model is the engine; Codex is the place where the engine can act.

The catch is trust. GPT-5.5 appears to know more, but the most reader-relevant warning is that it still answers too often when it does not know. For medical, legal, financial, client-facing, or operationally important work, the new model needs explicit uncertainty checks and human verification.

Capability desk

What actually got better

Codex became the main stage. GPT-5.5 did not arrive alone. The launch landed alongside Codex 0.124.0, which makes the release feel less like a model swap and more like a product move toward working agents.

The new Codex can open web apps, click through flows, fill forms, take screenshots, and iterate visually. That matters because many real tasks fail outside the code editor: a button is misplaced, a form does not submit, a PDF contains the decisive detail, or the app only breaks after a full user flow.

Document viewing is part of the same story. If the agent can read a PDF, inspect an artifact, and keep that material in the same working loop, then "rewrite this report" or "check this source" becomes less dependent on copy-paste from the user.

One launch-week anecdote captures the pitch better than any feature list: a user asked Codex to build a Mac app while he slept and woke up to working code. Treat that as a signal, not a guarantee. The point is that the frontier moved toward longer runs that can produce visible work.

Not prettier chat. Longer runs that can move through tools and finish visible work.

Price desk

Top of the cost curve

GPT-5.5 is a premium model, and the sticker price makes that explicit. OpenAI argues that the model uses fewer tokens on some workloads, so the final bill is not as simple as price per token. That may be true for long agent runs, but it does not remove the need to route expensive work carefully.

The practical reading is simple: GPT-5.5 should earn its place. Use it where the extra capability changes the outcome, not where a cheaper model already does the job. The table below is less a leaderboard than a buying guide.

Market desk

The launch was not a solo event

GPT-5.5 landed inside a crowded week. Claude Opus 4.7 had shipped a week earlier, DeepSeek answered on price, and Anthropic used the same news window to explain and fix quality problems in Claude Code. Google then answered financially, through Anthropic rather than through a Gemini release.

That context matters because it keeps the launch in proportion. GPT-5.5 is important, but it is not the whole market moving in one direction. The frontier is splitting into different lanes: premium autonomy, careful design work, research-heavy synthesis, and low-cost long-context processing.

Why these two?

The launch-week takes split less on "is it smart?" and more on where it fits. That is why Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 both matter in the same paper.

Opus 4.7 stays in the mix for design-heavy frontend work and careful document reasoning. GPT-5.5 earns its slot when the job is long, tool-heavy, and needs Codex to keep moving. The point is not loyalty to one lab; it is choosing the model whose failure mode you can live with.

Monday playbook

Choose the lane, then tighten the prompt

For daily use, the change is less dramatic than the launch language. You do not need GPT-5.5 for every quick answer. You do need clearer instructions when the stakes rise, because the model is more willing to infer a plan from vague intent.

If the work isLong codingUse GPT-5.5 when persistence, tools, and logic matter most.
If the work isResearchCompare Gemini first for science-heavy synthesis and multi-paper work.
If the work isFrontend designKeep Opus 4.7 in the race for visual and design-heavy UI tasks.
If the work isBudget scaleTest DeepSeek V4-Flash where context is large and cost dominates.
  • Ask for uncertainty explicitly on medical, legal, financial, client-facing, or regulated work. Treat confident detail as a thing to verify.
  • Default to lower reasoning for ordinary tasks and raise effort only when the task is truly hard. Otherwise it may overwork simple requests.
  • Benchmark before standardizing on your own prompts, documents, codebase, and acceptance criteria.
  • Tighten prompts. GPT-5.5 listens closely and may turn vague intent into a large plan. Say what should be changed, what should not, and how much output you want.
