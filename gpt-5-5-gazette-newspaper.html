GPT-5.5 wants the whole job

GPT-5.5 is not just a better chatbot story. The first 24 hours point to a shift toward agents that open tools, read documents, inspect screens, and keep working.

By the automated research deskDateline: T+24 hours

The main story is not raw intelligence. It is execution.

OpenAI sold GPT-5.5 as a model for real work, not just sharper answers. The question is whether GPT-5.5, Codex, and the new tool surface can turn a loose request into finished work without too much babysitting.

That makes the Codex release around it just as important as the model card. Browser use, document viewing, screenshots, plugins, and guarded long runs are the pieces that make the agent story concrete.

Read on for the practical version: what shipped, where the warning label is real, why the price changes the decision, and which tasks deserve GPT-5.5 first.

Capability desk

What actually got better

Codex became the main stage. GPT-5.5 did not arrive alone: the release was paired with Codex 0.124.0, turning the model story into a working-agent product story.

Browser work moved inside the agent. Codex can open web apps, click, fill forms, take screenshots, and iterate visually instead of only editing code from the outside.

Documents became first-class context. Native PDF and document viewing lets the agent read artifacts inline while it plans, edits, and checks the work.

Long runs got supervision. Auto-review adds a guardian agent that evaluates risky actions before execution, reducing approval stalls without making the run fully unattended.

Why these two?

The launch-week takes split less on "is it smart?" and more on where it fits. That is why Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 both matter in the same paper.

"Opus for design, GPT-5.5 for logic."Working developer heuristic
"A fast, effective and highly capable model."Simon Willison
"One impressive step on the curve."Ethan Mollick

Opus 4.7 stays in the mix for design-heavy frontend work and careful document reasoning. GPT-5.5 earns its slot when the job is long, tool-heavy, and needs Codex to keep moving. The point is not loyalty to one lab; it is choosing the model whose failure mode you can live with.

What to do Monday

Route the work, do not worship the model

If the work isLong codingUse GPT-5.5 when persistence, tools, and logic matter most.
If the work isResearchCompare Gemini first for science-heavy synthesis and multi-paper work.
If the work isFrontend designKeep Opus 4.7 in the race for visual and design-heavy UI tasks.
If the work isBudget scaleTest DeepSeek V4-Flash where context is large and cost dominates.
  • Ask for uncertainty explicitly on medical, legal, financial, client-facing, or regulated work. Treat confident detail as a thing to verify.
  • Default to lower reasoning for ordinary tasks and raise effort only when the task is truly hard. Otherwise it may overwork simple requests.
  • Benchmark before standardizing on your own prompts, documents, codebase, and acceptance criteria.
  • Tighten prompts. GPT-5.5 listens closely and may turn vague intent into a large plan. Say what should be changed, what should not, and how much output you want.
